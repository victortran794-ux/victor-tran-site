IBM Patterns: Contact Us

A six-week IBM Patterns sprint reimagining the flagship Contact Us experience on IBM.com so high-volume visitors could better self-route to the right team.

2021 · IBM Patterns · Team Rogue
01 · The Problem

A page built to look the part, not do the job

Massive traffic, unclear routing.

IBM's Contact Us page was drawing huge volume, but the experience was not reliably helping visitors reach the right destination. Over six months, the page logged 135,000 unique visitors and 13,000 form submissions, many of which were difficult to route cleanly across sales, support, careers, partners, and other IBM teams.

Multiple competing contact pages across IBM.com each claimed to be an entry point. The result was a page that looked like it belonged on IBM.com, but did not fully support what users came there to do: quickly understand their options and choose the right path forward.

02 · The Program

IBM Patterns

IBM Patterns is a six-week design education program that brings early-career IBM designers together under mentorship to tackle a real sponsored project using Enterprise Design Thinking. The format moves quickly: research, framing, prototyping, testing, and a final playback to IBM leadership.

Our team of five took on Contact Us: a high-traffic experience that had been treated as a general entry point, but needed to behave more like a routing system.

03 · Research & Framing

Two personas, one wall

We built two personas to anchor the research: Bradley, a developer at a large IBM customer account trying to resolve a technical issue, and Paige, a small business CEO looking to speak to a sales representative. Both hit the same wall: a generic form with no clear path forward, limited routing logic, and little confidence in what would happen next.

User research surfaced the same tension from multiple directions. People expected a fast, self-directed experience. Instead, the page pushed many visitors toward a contact form too early, before helping them understand whether sales, support, documentation, careers, partners, or another route was the better fit. One user put it plainly: "There is no excuse to not do personalized experiences for someone who has been to IBM.com before."

How might we help visitors contact the right team at IBM, quickly routing sales or support inquiries and diverting low-quality form submissions?

04 · The Design

Every path had a destination

Side navigation, clear subcategories, no dead ends.

We restructured the entire contact experience around a persistent side navigation: Support, Sales, IBM Careers, Learn, Business Partners, Additional Resources, and Contact Information. Each unfolded into clear subcategories with direct CTAs. No ambiguity about where a given request would land.

The landing page led with a hero ("Contact IBM. We're here to help.") backed by a photo to establish warmth and trust before routing users through topic-based icons. Sales inquiries were flagged for virtual agent routing. Developers could self-direct to documentation. The goal was to make every major path feel intentional instead of dumping everyone into the same form.

05 · Testing & Iteration

The structure held

User testing confirmed that the structure made sense. Participants found the category breakdown intuitive, and it better reflected their mental models for how to reach IBM. The hero image increased trust, while the mix of direct contact options and self-service routes supported different user needs without forcing everyone down the same path.

Based on feedback, we refined tertiary labels, tightened the form experience, and clarified where each path would lead before locking the MVP direction.

06 · Outcome

A page that finally did its job

The MVP direction brought together new primary category icons, a frequently requested information section, and clearer sales inquiry routing to a virtual agent. Short-term, the work aimed to reduce misdirected submissions, clarify paths for revenue-generating inquiries, and give technical users a self-service track when documentation was the better answer.

Long-term, the structure created a foundation for a more unified global IBM contact experience, including future opportunities for personalization. The contact page had been treated like a static destination; we reframed it as a routing experience.
