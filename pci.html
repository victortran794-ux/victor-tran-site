Principles of Business Conduct

A complete redesign of PCI's hourly-employee handbook.

Forty-two pages cover material ranging from leadership context to workplace policy and ethical standards. The design challenge was to give dense legal and HR content a clearer typographic structure for hourly employees.

Role: As a freelance designer, I created the layout, typography, illustration, and production design while working within PCI's existing brand.

Key Work: Extended PCI's established color and brand elements into a type hierarchy, hexagon motif, section dividers, and flexible grid for long-form policy text, sidebars, and supporting callouts.

Note: body copy is intentionally blurred in the spreads below to protect confidential HR content. The design system, hierarchy, and craft remain visible.

The CEO's Letter

The opening spread features a portrait of PCG President & CEO Jason Hendricks alongside a personal note about the company's principles. A red gradient washes across the page to anchor the brand color and frame the portrait against the hexagon pattern that runs throughout the book.

Interior Spreads

Each section uses oversized red display type, generous whitespace, and pull-quoted callouts to break up dense policy language. Hexagon watermarks and "Learn More" buttons signal where employees can dig deeper.

National Footprint

The back of the book visualizes PCI's national branch network through state-coded hexagons, extending the publication's graphic system into a geographic overview.

Banner System

The same hexagon language explored across recruitment and event concepts.

A companion concept system for PCI's HR and recruitment teams extended the hexagon language into standing banners, table displays, event graphics, and related environmental applications.

Key Work: Created the typography exploration, color and pattern studies, standing-banner and table-display mockups, and "Build the Future" concept art.

Outcome

The 2026 handbook was distributed to hourly employees company-wide. The banner and table-display directions remained concepts created for PCI's teams to use as flexible recruitment and event resources. Together, the work formed an unofficial internal extension of PCI's existing brand rather than a corporate rebrand.
